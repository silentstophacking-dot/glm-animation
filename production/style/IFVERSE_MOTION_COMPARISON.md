## Finding
The image style has moved closer to the reference than the animation performance. The accepted body build, visible neck/hands, detailed hero prop, sunset palette and selective focus form a useful baseline. The current movement is not yet approved for long-form production.

1. Walking remains mechanical. Our pelvis lowers once before the walk, stays low through most of the steps, then rises at the stop. Both knee chains bend in the same general direction while the torso stays upright. The reference's visible wide stride shows a clearer extended support leg/trailing leg relationship, more distinct footwear and a stronger arm silhouette. Fix the support/pass/contact poses, weight transfer and heel/toe articulation before polishing faster movement.
2. Acting vocabulary is too narrow. Our head tilts and smile/surprise switch carry most of the acting. In the reference interaction, face direction and body/arm gesture change with the other character. Add target-led gaze, true head-facing changes, asymmetric shoulders and a torso-led reaction. Do not replace a head turn with rotation of a circle and face marks.
3. Hands satisfy contact geometry but show limited performance. The reference interaction uses distinct held and released gestures. Our palm reaches the correct button anchor, but the same compact shape carries most of the action and the button gives little visible physical response. Add wrist preparation, clear touch/press/release poses and subtle button travel tied to the fingertip.
4. Camera sizes change, but viewpoints remain on the same axis. The reference selections combine establishing, two-character interaction and close shots with different staging. Our cuts mostly enlarge the same planar setup. Add a purposeful object insert and a face-focused reaction while preserving object positions and screen direction.
5. Lighting and props are closer, but the reference has richer scene-specific depth and surface treatment. Our blur, gradients and foreground overlap help, while many surfaces still have uniform shading. Keep material refinement subordinate to the acting and contact point. The detailed console is a reusable asset, not a reason to fill every background with equally sharp detail.

## Next priority
Retain the current design and material direction. Revise the gait and coordinated upper-body motion first, then directional gaze/head turns and hand/button performance. Review another short motion proof with the same comparison method. Full-film rebuild remains on hold.
